# Round 2 · 挪了下车 停车 Puzzle 原型报告

> Model slug: `claude-opus-5-thinking-high-fast`
> Deliverable 2 of the Round 2 brief — 新原型（挪了下车 parking puzzle）
> Branch `agent/wechat-minigames-research` · `prototypes/parking-jam/`

## TL;DR

Round 1 scored 挪了下车 at **9/10 replicability**, the highest of the blended top
ten, and predicted "1k–1.5k LOC core loop, BFS solver mandatory". Both hold up.
The playable game is **1,601 lines** of vanilla JS/HTML across four files, of
which the **BFS solver is 49**. Eight levels, win and loss states, move counter
with a budget, par-based stars, undo, rewarded-video-gated hints, two-gate and
multi-target variants, wired to the Round 2 shared `wx` shim. 23 unit tests plus
a headless verifier that completes every level through the shipped input
handlers. Zero dependencies, zero build step, zero imported art.

The one Round 1 claim worth sharpening: the solver is cheap and *level
generation* is expensive, but not for the reason the phrase "BFS solver" implies.
Search cost is trivial (under 10,000 states, under 100 ms for the hardest board).
The expense is **yield**: random placement almost never produces a hard board, so
a 34-move level cost roughly 25,000 generate-and-test attempts while a 9-move
level cost tens.

### Mechanic

Axis-constrained sliding on an occupancy grid, which is the Rush Hour family the
Round 1 analysis identified. Two deliberate extensions toward the parking-lot
fantasy and away from a plain Rush Hour clone:

1. **Departing cars are removed from the lot**, freeing their stalls. Rush Hour
   ends the moment its single red car escapes, so this only becomes meaningful
   with —
2. **Multiple targets and multiple gates.** Level 3 has two gold cars leaving
   through two different walls, and the first departure opens the lane for the
   second.

Both fell out of the data model rather than being bolted on: exits are `{axis,
dir, line}` wall gaps and win is "every target has left", so a one-target,
one-gate board is just the degenerate case.

## Findings

### 1. The solver is the product, and it is 49 lines

BFS over board states keyed by a positional fingerprint. The shortest-solution
length it returns is used four ways, which is why Round 1 was right to call it
mandatory rather than a debugging convenience:

- **difficulty rating** — orders the pack, and is the accept/reject test in the
  generator;
- **star par** — 3 stars at par, 2 within half again, 1 inside the budget;
- **move budget** — `limitForPar(par)`, about 1.8× par, which is what creates the
  loss state at all;
- **hint** — the first move of a current shortest solution.

Because it is this cheap, the hint solves the whole puzzle from scratch on the
device every time it is pressed. No precomputed solution ships with a level, and
a hint stays correct after arbitrary player moves — including moves that make the
board *harder* than the authored line.

### 2. Search cost and par decouple

Level 6 needs 19 moves and explores 9,368 states; level 8 needs 34 moves and
explores 9,294. Search cost tracks how *tangled* the lot is — how many
independent cars have somewhere to go — while par tracks how long the single
escape line is. Practical consequence for a content pipeline: **you cannot use
search cost as a difficulty proxy**, even though it is the number that falls out
of the solver for free. Only solution length correlates with perceived
difficulty.

### 3. Generate-and-test yield collapses at the top of the ramp

Measured on this generator, 6×6, 10–14 cars:

| Target par | Boards tried before a hit |
|---|---|
| 8–9 | tens |
| 12–13 | tens |
| 17–19 | low hundreds |
| 23–26 | ~3,600 |
| ≥ 26 | ~9,000 |
| 34 | ~25,000 (≈ 4 min single-threaded) |

This is the real shape of the content problem, and it reframes Round 1's
"thousands of levels with a monotonic difficulty ramp" note. The first few
hundred easy levels are free. The last few hundred hard ones are not, and a
production pipeline would need reverse construction from a solved state, or
local search that perturbs a known-hard board, to get them at a sane cost.
Difficulty *rating* is solved; difficulty *targeting* is not.

### 4. Mechanic vs. product, on the Round 2 dual axis

Adding the Round 2 dual-axis framing to this one title:

| Axis | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---:|---|
| **Mechanic replicability** | **9/10** | Confirmed by construction. 1,601 lines, minutes of compute, no engine, no server, no assets. The missing point is the content pipeline in finding 3, not the game |
| **Product replicability** | **3/10** | The loop is a step-limited puzzle, which makes the hint the single monetised moment — and the hint is a rewarded video, so the revenue line *is* `wx.createRewardedVideoAd`. There is no version of this outside WeChat's ad network |

The gap between the two axes is unusually wide for this title, wider than for
羊了个羊. A tile-matcher at least has a shareable failure state ("分享复活"); a
parking jam's failure state is running out of moves, which players resolve with a
hint rather than a share. That concentrates the entire business model into one
platform API.

### 5. The hint *is* the business model, so the ad gate is not decoration

This prototype consumes deliverable 3, `prototypes/shared/wx-shim.js`.
`installWxShim()` publishes a mock `globalThis.wx` and steps aside when a real
WeChat host is present, so the calls in `src/main.js` are the calls a mini-game
build makes:

| Capability | WeChat API | Behaviour here |
|---|---|---|
| Rewarded video for a hint | `wx.createRewardedVideoAd` | Real gate: 提示 shows a 3-second player; watch it through and the hint appears, skip and it does not, no-fill grants it anyway |
| Share card | `wx.shareAppMessage` | Fires with the level in `query`; the shim simulates a friend opening it |
| Best scores | `wx.setUserCloudStorage` / `getUserCloudStorage` | Written to cloud KV, cached locally for the HUD |

Wiring it produced the sharpest product-side observation of this exercise, and
it is a genre difference rather than a detail. A tile-matcher's monetised moment
is **分享复活** — a share, which is free inventory and viral by construction. A
step-limited parking puzzle has no revive to share; when you are stuck you are
stuck, and the only thing left to sell is the answer. So the entire revenue line
collapses onto one API, `wx.createRewardedVideoAd`, with no viral side effect to
compensate. That is the concrete mechanism behind the 3/10 product score above,
and it is why `limitForPar` — the move budget coefficient — is the single most
commercially sensitive constant in the codebase: it sets how often a player gets
stuck, which is how often an ad plays.

Porting the rest means swapping the DOM HUD for canvas-drawn widgets and mapping
`pointerdown/move/up` onto `wx.onTouchStart/Move/End`. `core.js` compiles
unchanged — it touches no DOM, no canvas and no `wx.*`.

### 6. Testing the shipped UI caught what testing the core could not

`verify.js` boots the real `main.js`, `render.js` and the shared shim against a
stub DOM and completes all eight levels by synthesising pointer drags through
the real handlers, with a virtual clock driving the animations. That immediately
surfaced a bug no core-level test could reach: the best-score readout refreshed
before the win was recorded, so a new personal best appeared one level late.

Driving the animations from `requestAnimationFrame` rather than `setTimeout`
turned out to be what makes this work. The virtual clock advances rAF frames
on demand, so slides, the exit animation and even the 3-second rewarded-video
countdown all complete deterministically inside a synchronous test — including
the assertion that skipping the video actually denies the hint.

Recommendation for deliverable 4 (unified headless runner): **make stub-DOM
playthrough the standard, not solver-only checks.** `tile-trio/verify.js`
already does this for a single-file build; this prototype shows the same pattern
works for a multi-module ES build, and it is where the bugs actually are.


Deep-link a level with `index.html#8`.

## Known gaps

- No WeChat device or SDK verification (Linux environment, unchanged from
  Round 1).
- The generator only emits single-target, right-wall boards. The multi-target
  and multi-gate variants are hand-authored; generalising the generator to them
  is straightforward but was not needed for eight levels.
- No sound, no juice beyond slide easing and a bump animation.
- `limitForPar` (1.8×) is picked, not tuned against player data. In a live title
  this coefficient is the single biggest lever on hint-ad impressions per
  session, and would be A/B tested.
